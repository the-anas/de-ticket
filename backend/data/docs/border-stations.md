# Border stations and trips abroad

Starter content, checked 2026-10-11. Sources: ticket-rechner.de (updated 2026-07-21), bus-und-bahn.de, de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutschlandticket. Verify before travelling.

## Austria: Salzburg and Kufstein

Salzburg Hbf is covered when you arrive via Freilassing on a regional train (BRB, DB or ÖBB S-Bahn). From München the direct regional train takes about two hours. Kufstein is covered via Kiefersfelden on BRB trains from München or Rosenheim. Buses in Salzburg and onward trains into Austria (for example to Innsbruck or Vienna) are not covered. Westbahn and Railjet trains on the München–Salzburg line are long-distance trains and not valid.

## Austria: the Außerfern line through Reutte

The regional line Kempten – Pfronten – Reutte in Tirol – Ehrwald – Garmisch-Partenkirchen runs partly through Austria. The ticket is valid for through journeys between German stations and for trips starting or ending in Germany. Journeys wholly inside Austria, such as Reutte to Ehrwald, are not covered. It is a scenic way to link the Allgäu with Garmisch.

## Switzerland: Basel and Schaffhausen

Basel Badischer Bahnhof is a German-operated station on Swiss soil and is covered. Regional trains from Weil am Rhein and Lörrach also continue to Basel SBB. Schaffhausen is covered on the regional trains from Singen and Erzingen, and trains between Singen and Waldshut pass through it. Swiss trams, buses and onward trains need a Swiss ticket.

## France: Wissembourg, Lauterbourg and Sarreguemines

From the Palatinate, regional trains reach Wissembourg (via Winden and Schweighofen) and Lauterbourg (via Wörth and Berg). From Saarbrücken, the Saarbahn tram-train S1 runs to Sarreguemines (Saargemünd). saarVV buses also reach Creutzwald and Carling. The tram from Kehl to Strasbourg is not covered. In July and August 2026 there was a summer offer for under-28s living in Baden-Württemberg, Rhineland-Palatinate or Saarland covering regional trains in the Grand Est region.

## Netherlands: Venlo, Arnhem, Enschede and Hengelo

Venlo is covered via Kaldenkirchen (RE 13 from Düsseldorf and Mönchengladbach). Arnhem Centraal is covered via Emmerich (RE 19 from Düsseldorf). Enschede is covered via Gronau (RB 51 from Dortmund, RB 64 from Münster). Hengelo is covered via Bad Bentheim. Around Aachen, AVV buses reach Vaals and Kerkrade. Dutch trains and buses beyond these points need a Dutch ticket.

## Belgium and Luxembourg

In Belgium only Kelmis is covered, on AVV buses from Aachen; trains to Belgium are not included. For Luxembourg, the regional trains on the Trier – Wasserbillig – Luxembourg line (including CFL trains) and several cross-border bus lines were reported as covered until 31 December 2026 under a pilot. Public transport inside Luxembourg is free for everyone anyway. The Saarbrücken–Luxembourg express bus is not covered.

## Denmark: Tønder

Tønder is covered only on the RB 66 from Niebüll. There is no valid route from Flensburg into Denmark, and the Puttgarden–Rødby ferry is not included.

## Poland: Świnoujście and Zgorzelec

Świnoujście Centrum on Usedom is the last stop of the Usedomer Bäderbahn from Züssow and Heringsdorf and is covered; the beach and town centre are within walking distance. Zgorzelec, across the Neisse from Görlitz, is covered by the regional train from Görlitz, and Krzewina Zgorzelecka is a stop on the Görlitz–Zittau line that lies on the Polish bank. Trains to Szczecin, Wrocław or Poznań are not covered beyond the last German tariff point.

## Czech Republic: Hrádek, Varnsdorf and Vejprty

Trilex trains from Zittau pass through Hrádek nad Nisou and Varnsdorf on their way to Liberec and Seifhennersdorf; the ticket is valid to those two Czech stations. Vejprty is covered from Bärenstein in the Ore Mountains. Děčín, Ústí and Prague need a Czech ticket from the border.
