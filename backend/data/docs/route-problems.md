# Known problems and awkward routes

Starter content, written 2026-10-11 from general knowledge of the network. Construction works change constantly; always check the current timetable (the route search uses live timetable data) and bauinfos.deutschebahn.com before a long trip.

## Long north–south journeys take a full day

Crossing Germany on regional trains is possible but slow. Berlin to München takes around 9 to 10 hours with five or more changes, against about 4 hours on the ICE. Hamburg to München is 12 hours or more. Plan such trips with a buffer, or split them with an overnight stop in a city on the way, for example Leipzig, Nürnberg, Erfurt or Würzburg.

## Tight connections and missed transfers

Regional connections are often planned with 5 to 10 minute transfers, and one late train breaks the chain. With the Deutschlandticket there is no reserved train, so you simply take the next one, but on lines that run every two hours this costs a lot of time. Prefer connections with at least 10 minutes at large stations, and avoid relying on the last connection of the day.

## Crowded trains at weekends and holidays

Since the ticket was introduced, popular leisure lines are very full on sunny weekends and public holidays, especially Hamburg–Westerland (Sylt), Berlin–Baltic coast (RE 3, RE 5 to Stralsund and Rostock), München–Garmisch and München–Salzburg, Köln–Koblenz along the Rhine, and Dresden–Bad Schandau. Trains can be too full to board with a bike. Travel early in the morning or on weekdays where possible.

## Corridor overhauls (Generalsanierung)

Deutsche Bahn is closing busy main lines one at a time for several months each to renew them completely; Frankfurt–Mannheim (2024) and Hamburg–Berlin (from August 2025) were the first, and more corridors follow each year. During a closure, regional trains are replaced by buses or diverted. Replacement buses (Schienenersatzverkehr, SEV) are covered by the Deutschlandticket but are slower and cannot take many bikes. If a planned route shows a bus leg on what is normally a rail line, expect this to be the reason.

## Replacement buses and strike days

Rail replacement buses run on the published timetable and appear in route searches as bus legs. During strikes by train drivers or signal staff, regional service can be reduced to an emergency timetable; private operators (BRB, ODEG, Metronom and others) are often unaffected when the strike targets DB only. City transport strikes affect buses, trams and U-Bahn but usually not the S-Bahn.

## Rural lines with sparse service

Branch lines and rural buses may run only every one or two hours, and some have no evening or Sunday service. This matters for the last leg to places like Rothenburg ob der Tauber, Monschau, or villages in the Eifel, the Bavarian Forest and Mecklenburg. Check the return connection before setting out, not only the outbound one.

## Border crossings are narrower than they look

A route search can show a regional train continuing across the border beyond the last covered station, for example München–Kufstein–Innsbruck or Dresden–Bad Schandau–Děčín. The Deutschlandticket ends at the listed border station (Kufstein) or at the last German station (Schöna on the Dresden line), and you need a ticket for the foreign section bought before boarding.
