# Deutschlandticket rules

Starter content, checked 2026-10-11. Sources: de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutschlandticket, verbraucherzentrale.de, bahn.de.

## What the ticket is and what it costs

The Deutschlandticket (D-Ticket) is a monthly subscription for local and regional public transport across Germany. Since January 2026 it costs 63 euros per month; 66.80 euros is planned from January 2027. It is sold as a subscription that can be cancelled monthly, is personal and non-transferable, and is issued digitally (app or chip card). Carry photo ID, because inspectors may ask for it.

## Which transport is included

The Deutschlandticket is valid in 2nd class on all local public transport (ÖPNV) in Germany: buses, trams, U-Bahn, S-Bahn, and regional trains. Regional train categories include RB (Regionalbahn), RE (Regional-Express), IRE (Interregio-Express), MEX (Metropolexpress), S-Bahn, and the regional trains of private operators such as BRB, ODEG, Metronom, eurobahn, National Express, vlexx, erixx, trilex, agilis, Go-Ahead and Arverio. It is valid at any time of day, with no limit on the number of journeys.

## Which trains are not included

Long-distance trains are not included in the Deutschlandticket: ICE, IC, EC, ECE, Railjet (RJ/RJX), Nightjet (NJ) and other night trains, TGV, and Westbahn. FlixTrain and FlixBus are not included, and neither are other long-distance coaches. First class is not included; some transport associations sell a first-class add-on. Tourist and heritage services such as steam railways (for example the Harz narrow-gauge Brockenbahn) and many cable cars usually need their own ticket.

## Long-distance trains that do accept the ticket

A handful of IC/ICE sections accept the Deutschlandticket because the long-distance train replaces regional service there. As of mid-2026 these were: Rostock – Stralsund (IC and ICE); Bremen – Oldenburg – Leer – Emden – Norddeich Mole / Emden Außenhafen (IC); Stuttgart – Horb – Singen – Konstanz on the Gäubahn (IC); Dresden – Freiberg – Chemnitz (IC); Dortmund – Siegen – Dillenburg (only specific IC trains); Erfurt – Weimar – Jena – Gera (IC). The list changes at timetable changes, so check the Deutsche Bahn page "Anerkennung von Nahverkehrstickets in Intercity-Zügen" before relying on it. This app's route search does not offer these trains; it only plans regional services.

## Ferries and boats

The Deutschlandticket is valid on some ferries that are part of a city network, notably the HVV harbour ferries in Hamburg and the BVG ferries in Berlin. Most other ferries and boats are not included: the ferries to Borkum, Föhr and Amrum, the Loreley ferry, and most Rhine and lake excursion boats need a separate ticket. On the Lake Constance car ferry the ticket only covers bus passengers. Treat any ferry leg in a plan as needing confirmation with the operator.

## Bikes, dogs and travelling companions

The Deutschlandticket covers one person only: it does not include a bike, a dog or a travelling companion. Bikes need a separate bike ticket where the local tariff requires one, and bike carriage can be restricted at peak times. Dogs follow the local tariff: small dogs in a carrier are usually free, larger dogs often need a child or dog ticket. Children under 6 travel free on regional trains in general; older children need their own ticket.

## Using the ticket abroad

Outside Germany the Deutschlandticket is valid only to a short list of border stations, because the German tariff area ends at a station rather than at the border line. Beyond those stations you need a ticket of the neighbouring country. Local transport in the foreign town (for example trams in Basel or buses in Salzburg) is not covered. Each neighbouring country has its own section in the document on border stations, which lists the stations and the lines that reach them.
