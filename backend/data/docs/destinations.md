# Destinations worth the ride

Starter content, written 2026-10-11 from general travel knowledge. Travel times are rough and depend on the connection; use the route search for actual times.

## Rhine and Moselle valleys

The Rhine and Moselle valleys are a classic day trip by regional train from Köln, Bonn, Koblenz, Mainz or Frankfurt. The Upper Middle Rhine between Koblenz and Bingen is a UNESCO landscape of castles and vineyards, and regional trains run on both banks about every hour. Sit on the river side: the left-bank line (RB 26 / RE 2 Koblenz–Mainz) passes Boppard, St. Goar, Oberwesel and Bacharach. Good stops are Bacharach for half-timbered lanes and Boppard for the chairlift and river bend. On the Moselle, the RE and RB trains between Koblenz and Trier stop at Cochem, with the Reichsburg above the town. Trier itself has the Porta Nigra and Roman baths. Rhine excursion boats are not included in the ticket.

German keywords: Rhein, Rheintal, Mittelrhein, Mittelrheintal, Mosel, Moseltal, Burgen, Schlösser, Weinberge, Wein, Fachwerk, Schifffahrt, Ausflug

## Bavarian Alps and lakes

The Bavarian Alps and their lakes are the classic day trip by regional train from München. Garmisch-Partenkirchen is about 80 minutes from München by regional train and is the base for the Partnach gorge and the Zugspitze (the Zugspitze cog railway and cable car need their own ticket). Berchtesgaden, reached via Freilassing, gives access to the Königssee by local bus, which is included. Füssen, two hours from München, is the stop for Neuschwanstein; the bus to Hohenschwangau is included. Mittenwald, Tegernsee and Kochel are easy day trips from München on BRB and DB trains.

German keywords: Alpen, bayerische Alpen, Bayern, Oberbayern, Berge, Seen, Wandern, Schloss Neuschwanstein, Zugspitzbahn, Seilbahn, Klamm, Ausflug

## Lake Constance

Lake Constance (Bodensee), on the border with Switzerland and Austria, is a long day trip or a weekend by regional train from Stuttgart, Ulm or München. In Lindau, the Lindau-Insel station is on the island itself. Konstanz is reached by the Schwarzwaldbahn from Offenburg or from Singen, and Friedrichshafen and Meersburg (by bus) sit on the north shore. The Bodensee belt line links Lindau, Friedrichshafen, Überlingen and Radolfzell. Lake boats and the Konstanz–Meersburg car ferry are generally not included.

German keywords: Bodensee, See, Insel, Fähre, Schiff, Bodenseegürtelbahn, Ausflug

## Black Forest

The Black Forest is the wooded mountain region in the south-west, a day trip by regional train from Freiburg, Karlsruhe, Stuttgart or Basel. Freiburg is the main gateway. The Höllentalbahn climbs from Freiburg to Titisee and on to Schluchsee or Donaueschingen. The Schwarzwaldbahn from Offenburg to Konstanz via Triberg is one of the most scenic regional lines in Germany, with dozens of tunnels. Guest cards (KONUS) are separate from the Deutschlandticket and not needed if you hold it.

German keywords: Schwarzwald, Wandern, Wald, Berge, Tunnel, Gästekarte, Ausflug, Urlaub

## Saxon Switzerland and Dresden

Saxon Switzerland is the sandstone hiking area on the Elbe just outside Dresden: an easy day trip from Dresden and a longer one from Leipzig. From Dresden the S1 runs along the Elbe to Kurort Rathen (ferry across to the Bastei path; the small ferry needs its own ticket), Königstein and Bad Schandau in 30 to 45 minutes. The Kirnitzschtal tram from Bad Schandau is a local line with its own tariff. Dresden itself has the Frauenkirche, Zwinger and the Elbe meadows, and Meissen is 35 minutes away on the S1 in the other direction.

German keywords: Sächsische Schweiz, Sachsen, Elbsandsteingebirge, Elbe, Wandern, Bastei, Fähre, Meißen, Ausflug

## Harz

The Harz is a low mountain range in central Germany, a day or weekend trip by regional train from Hannover, Braunschweig, Magdeburg, Halle or Leipzig. Quedlinburg, Wernigerode and Goslar are half-timbered towns on the northern edge of the Harz, all on regional lines from Halle, Magdeburg, Hannover and Braunschweig. The Harz narrow-gauge steam railways (HSB), including the Brockenbahn to the summit, do not accept the Deutschlandticket. Thale, for the Bode gorge and Hexentanzplatz, is a short ride from Quedlinburg.

German keywords: Fachwerk, Fachwerkstädte, Schmalspurbahn, Harzer Schmalspurbahnen, Dampflok, Dampfzug, Brocken, Wandern, Ausflug

## Baltic coast

The Baltic coast is the nearest seaside by regional train from Hamburg and Berlin, with beaches, islands and brick old towns. Lübeck has a brick Gothic old town and is 45 minutes from Hamburg; Travemünde beach is a further 20 minutes. Warnemünde is at the end of the Rostock S-Bahn. Stralsund is the gateway to Rügen, where regional trains continue to Binz and Sassnitz. Usedom is served by the Usedomer Bäderbahn from Züssow to Heringsdorf, Ahlbeck and Świnoujście in Poland. The Rasender Roland steam railway on Rügen needs its own ticket.

German keywords: Ostsee, Ostseeküste, Küste, Meer, Strand, Strandurlaub, Baden, Insel, Rügen, Usedom, Altstadt, Backsteingotik, Urlaub, Ausflug

## North Sea coast and Sylt

The North Sea coast and its islands are reached by regional train from Hamburg and Bremen. Regional trains from Hamburg-Altona run to Westerland on Sylt over the Hindenburgdamm in about three hours; the line is single-track in places and delays are common in summer. Husum and Sankt Peter-Ording are on the same corridor. On the Lower Saxony coast, Norddeich Mole is the ferry port for Norderney and Juist, and Cuxhaven is at the end of the line from Hamburg. Island ferries are not included in the Deutschlandticket.

German keywords: Nordsee, Nordseeküste, Küste, Meer, Strand, Insel, Inseln, Fähre, Wattenmeer, Nordfriesland, Ostfriesland, Urlaub, Ausflug

## Franconia and the Romantic Road

Franconia, in northern Bavaria, has some of the best-preserved old towns in Germany and is a day trip by regional train from Nürnberg, Würzburg, München or Frankfurt. Bamberg, Würzburg and Nürnberg are linked by frequent regional expresses. Bamberg's old town is a UNESCO site and Würzburg has the Residenz. Rothenburg ob der Tauber is reached with a change at Steinach (bei Rothenburg) onto a branch line. Nördlingen and Dinkelsbühl (bus) are further south along the Romantic Road. Regensburg and Passau on the Danube are reached from Nürnberg or München.

German keywords: Franken, Romantische Straße, Altstadt, Weltkulturerbe, Donau, Bayern, Ausflug

## Thuringia and the classical towns

The classical towns of Thuringia are a day or weekend trip by regional train from Leipzig, Halle or Erfurt. Erfurt, Weimar, Jena and Eisenach lie on one east-west line with frequent regional trains. Weimar is a compact town for Goethe, Schiller and the Bauhaus museum; Eisenach has the Wartburg (a steep walk or a local bus from the station). Erfurt's Krämerbrücke and cathedral hill are a 15-minute walk from the station.

German keywords: Thüringen, Klassik, Weimarer Klassik, Wartburg, Dom, Altstadt, Museum, Ausflug

## Big cities on one ticket

The Deutschlandticket is valid on the whole local network inside Berlin, Hamburg, München, Köln, Frankfurt, Stuttgart and every other German city, so it also works as a city pass: S-Bahn, U-Bahn, trams and buses, including airport S-Bahn lines. In Hamburg the harbour ferries (for example line 62 to Finkenwerder) are a cheap harbour cruise.

German keywords: Großstadt, Großstädte, Städte, Stadtverkehr, Nahverkehr, Straßenbahn, Flughafen, Hafenfähre, Hafenrundfahrt
